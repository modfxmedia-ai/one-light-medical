## How Excess Weight Disrupts Your Digestive System

Digestive issues aren’t just uncomfortable—they can be dangerous if ignored. **Excess weight** disrupts digestion in several ways, affecting everything from stomach acid regulation to nutrient absorption.

### Increased Abdominal Pressure

Carrying extra weight, particularly around your abdomen, increases pressure on your stomach and intestines. This can lead to **gastroesophageal reflux disease (GERD)**, where stomach acid flows back into your esophagus, causing burning, inflammation, and over time, **esophageal damage or ulcers** ().

Even mild pressure can worsen bloating, gas, and discomfort. Left untreated, this pressure contributes to long-term **structural and functional changes in your digestive tract**, making it harder to digest food efficiently.

### Altered Gut Microbiome

Obesity can negatively affect the **gut microbiome**, the ecosystem of bacteria critical to digestion and overall health. Excess fat produces inflammatory molecules called **cytokines**, which disrupt the balance of good bacteria and promote overgrowth of harmful bacteria.

This imbalance can:

- Slow digestion
- Increase gas and bloating
- Contribute to further weight gain
- Increase risk for metabolic issues such as diabetes

Without intervention, these gut microbiome disruptions worsen over time, leading to chronic digestive issues and **poor nutrient absorption**.

### Slowed Transit Time

Excess weight often slows intestinal motility, which means food moves more slowly through your digestive tract. This can result in:

- Chronic constipation
- Persistent bloating
- Increased risk for diverticulosis and other gastrointestinal disorders

Ignoring these symptoms can have cascading consequences, including malnutrition, fatigue, and heightened risk of long-term gastrointestinal disease.

## The Dire Consequences of Ignoring Digestive Issues

Digestive problems caused by weight gain aren’t just inconvenient—they **pose serious health risks**. Chronic GERD can cause **esophageal ulcers or precancerous changes**, while persistent constipation and bloating can lead to complications such as **hemorrhoids or intestinal obstruction**.

Research also shows a direct link between obesity and **increased risk of colon cancer** (). Chronic inflammation from excess weight damages tissues and can compromise your immune system, making it harder for your body to heal and maintain gut health.
