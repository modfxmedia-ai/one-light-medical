Low back pain has a way of showing up right when you want to be more active. Yardwork, road trips, ball games, and time with kids or grandkids all ask more from your back. Then out of nowhere, a sharp pinch or deep ache can make even simple movements hard.

## Sciatica vs. General Low Back Pain: What Is Really Going On?

General low back pain usually stays around the lower spine and nearby muscles. It may feel:

- Dull, achy, or tight
- Stiff when you first get up or after sitting too long
- Sore when you bend, twist, or lift

This type of pain often comes from strained muscles, irritated joints, or discs that are under stress but are not pressing strongly on a nerve.

True sciatica is different. The sciatic nerve is a large nerve that starts in the lower back, runs through the buttock, down the back of the thigh, into the calf, and sometimes into the foot. When a nerve root in the lower spine is pinched or irritated, pain can travel along this path.

Sciatica can feel:

- Sharp, electric, or shooting
- Burning, tingling, or like pins and needles
- Numb in spots, with or without weakness

Common causes for adults in Amarillo include disc bulges or herniations, spinal stenosis, tight hip muscles like the piriformis, long hours of sitting with poor posture, heavy or repetitive lifting, and older injuries that never healed well.

Calling every back flare “sciatica” can cause problems. If you treat a muscle strain like a nerve issue, you may rest too much and lose strength. If you treat a true nerve problem like a simple strain, you may push through the pain and keep the nerve irritated. The right label helps guide the right plan, so you can protect the spine and avoid long-term nerve changes.

## How to Tell If It’s Sciatica or General Back Pain

Symptom patterns can give helpful clues.

Sciatica often shows up as:

- Pain that shoots down one leg, often below the knee
- Electric, burning, or stabbing pain in the buttock, thigh, or calf
- Numbness, tingling, or weakness in part of the leg or foot
- Pain worse with sitting, coughing, sneezing, or straining

General low back pain tends to:

- Stay in the low back, sometimes on one or both sides
- Feel like a dull ache, stiffness, or tightness
- Flare after long periods of standing, bending, or lifting
- Ease with gentle movement and position changes

At home, you can notice a few simple things:

- Does the pain follow a clear line from the buttock down the back of the leg?
- Do you feel tingling, numbness, or clear weakness in your leg or foot?
- Does sitting make things worse, while standing or walking feels better, or the other way around?

Certain red-flag symptoms mean you should seek immediate medical care, not wait and see:

- Sudden or worsening leg weakness
- Loss of bladder or bowel control
- Pain after a fall, accident, or other trauma
- Fever or feeling very ill along with back pain

Even with these clues, only a proper exam can confirm what is really going on. Sometimes sciatica comes from a disc. Other times, it is more about joint alignment or muscle spasm. ## Choosing the Right Care Path for Your Back This Summer

It can be hard to know when to seek help and when to give your back a few days of rest. As a general guide, it is time for a professional evaluation if:

- Pain has lasted more than a week or two without clear improvement
- You have repeated flare-ups that keep coming back
- You notice pain, tingling, or numbness that travels into the leg
